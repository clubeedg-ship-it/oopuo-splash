Your company probably uses AI already. Maybe it's a chatbot handling customer inquiries. Maybe it's an algorithm scoring credit applications or filtering job candidates. Under the EU AI Act, all of these carry obligations — and most businesses haven't started preparing.

This guide breaks down what the regulation actually requires, who it applies to, and when the deadlines hit. No legal jargon, no fearmongering. Just what you need to know to make decisions.

## What the EU AI Act actually is

The EU AI Act is the world's first comprehensive law regulating artificial intelligence. It was formally adopted in 2024 and entered into force on 1 August 2024. It doesn't ban AI — it creates a framework of obligations based on how much risk an AI system poses.

Think of it like product safety regulation. The higher the risk your AI system poses to people's health, safety, or fundamental rights, the more obligations you carry.

## The risk categories

**Unacceptable risk** — banned outright. Social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance, manipulative AI.

**High risk** — heavily regulated. Hiring, credit scoring, insurance, education, critical infrastructure. Must meet data governance, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, and robustness requirements.

**Limited risk** — transparency obligations. Chatbots and deepfake generators must disclose AI interaction.

**Minimal risk** — no specific obligations. Spam filters, AI games, internal productivity tools.

## Who it applies to

The Act applies based on your role in the AI supply chain. Providers develop AI systems. Deployers use them professionally. If your company uses an AI hiring tool built by another vendor, you're a deployer with real obligations.

Using off-the-shelf AI doesn't exempt you from the law.

## The timeline

`February 2025` — Prohibitions on unacceptable-risk AI practices apply.
`August 2025` — Rules on general-purpose AI models apply.
`August 2026` — High-risk system requirements and deployer obligations go live.
`August 2027` — Extended deadline for certain regulated sectors.

## The penalties

`€35 million or 7%` of global annual turnover — for prohibited AI practices.
`€15 million or 3%` — for non-compliance with high-risk system obligations.
`€7.5 million or 1.5%` — for providing incorrect information to authorities.

## What you should do now

**Inventory your AI systems.** Document every AI tool your company uses — including third-party SaaS.

**Classify the risk.** Map each system against the Act's risk categories.

**Assess your role.** Provider, deployer, or both?

**Start documentation.** The Act requires extensive technical documentation for high-risk systems.

**Build observability.** Logging, tracing, and monitoring aren't optional for high-risk deployers.
