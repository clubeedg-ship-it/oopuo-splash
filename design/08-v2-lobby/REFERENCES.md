# REFERENCES — Operations Room Coming Online

Curated reference library for the v2 lobby concept: a marketing site that feels like an **operations room booting up** — equal parts game lobby, Japanese minimalism, and modern enterprise software. The visitor is an SMB owner. Every borrowed element has to land as "professional and modern," never "gamey" or "cold corporate."

Each entry below names a single **atomic element** — one visual or interaction idea worth cherry-picking — verified against a real source.

---

## 1. Game UI Lobbies / HUDs

The orbit screens, garages, and pause menus that turn navigation into a place. We borrow restraint from these, not chrome.

### 1.1 Destiny 2 — The Director (orbit screen)
- **Source:** Bungie / David Candland UX team
- **URL:** https://www.cand.land/destiny · https://www.gameuidatabase.com/gameData.php?id=175
- **Atomic element:** A **single radial map** that lets the user open it from anywhere and travel directly to any destination in one step — no menu trees. Selection feels like *pointing at a place*, not navigating IA.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Replaces a traditional "Services / About / Pricing" nav with one constellation view. SMB owners get an immediate map of the consultancy's universe instead of a dropdown.

### 1.2 Armored Core VI — Garage HUD
- **Source:** FromSoftware / Game UI Database
- **URL:** https://www.gameuidatabase.com/gameData.php?id=1826
- **Atomic element:** **Information clustered around a large empty central reticle** — vitals on the left, weapons on the right, status across the bottom, but the middle of the screen is *deliberately empty* so the subject (your mech / your project) breathes.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Center-empty layouts let a hero illustration or product render dominate while still surrounding it with operational metadata — looks serious without looking busy.

### 1.3 NieR: Automata — Chip equipping screen
- **Source:** PlatinumGames / Game UI Database
- **URL:** https://www.gameuidatabase.com/gameData.php?id=150 · https://interfaceingame.com/games/nierautomata/
- **Atomic element:** **A slim vertical inventory column** where each item is a colored striped bar consuming a discrete amount of vertical space — items literally take up "memory" you can see. Pure data, no skeuomorphism.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Translate to a vertical "capacity bar" or service stack — each engagement type as a horizontal stripe that *physically occupies* a column. Instantly readable as a system.

### 1.4 NieR: Automata — Pause menu typography
- **Source:** Fontworks (typeface "Rodin")
- **URL:** https://en.fontworks.co.jp/case/10971/
- **Atomic element:** **Wide letter-spaced labels in a clean Japanese sans, set on a single neutral field** (warm beige in NieR, but the principle ports). Text is always centered or left-aligned in a quiet column with massive surrounding whitespace.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Demonstrates that "menu" can feel meditative, not transactional. Borrow the idea of *one label per breath of space*.

### 1.5 Death Stranding — Cuff Links holographic map
- **Source:** Kojima Productions / interfaceingame.com
- **URL:** https://interfaceingame.com/games/death-stranding/
- **Atomic element:** **Topographic map projected as a translucent hologram** — single-color line work, contour-style elevation, status indicated by *color of the entire field* (blue = stable, yellow = caution).
- **Why for OOPUO:** A monochromatic line-art "operations map" of the client's business. Color-of-the-field as state indicator (current quarter teal, problem area warm) is more elegant than badges.

### 1.6 Cyberpunk 2077 — Curved HUD shader
- **Source:** CD PROJEKT RED / Behance UI manual
- **URL:** https://www.behance.net/gallery/133185623/Cyberpunk-2077User-Interface-(Part-2)
- **Atomic element:** A **subtle curvature applied to the entire HUD via shader** so flat panels sit on an invisible cylindrical surface. Edges feel *attached to a viewport*, not flat-pasted.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Used at 1–2% intensity, this gives a marketing site a faint sense of being viewed *through* a screen — the operations-room feeling without literal scanlines.

## 2. Japanese Minimalist Digital Design

These are our restraint references. They prevent the operations-room idea from becoming a sci-fi cosplay.

### 2.1 MUJI — "ma" (negative space) on the storefront
- **Source:** Kenya Hara, MUJI Art Director
- **URL:** https://blog.withdipp.com/en/the-art-of-less-how-japanese-minimalism-brands-muji-uniqlo-and-standard-products-define-success
- **Atomic element:** **Product photography floats inside a square of empty white** that is at least 60% of the tile area. The emptiness *is* the design — Hara calls this "emptiness" rather than minimalism.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Each service tile / case-study card needs the same 60% rule: imagery occupies less than half its container, the rest is signal-by-absence.

### 2.2 MUJI — Wide letter-spacing on sans labels
- **Source:** Humble Bunny / contemporary Japanese graphic design analysis
- **URL:** https://www.humblebunny.com/minimialism-japanese-creative-design/
- **Atomic element:** **Section labels set in a neutral sans with markedly wider tracking than Western defaults** (~0.05–0.15em on body sans, much higher on captions).
- **Why for OOPUO:** Apply tracking ~0.12em to your JetBrains Mono section labels — instantly reads as "considered" rather than "tech-bro mono."

### 2.3 Issey Miyake — Layout × typography by Ikko Tanaka
- **Source:** Miyake Design Studio
- **URL:** https://mds.isseymiyake.com/im/en/
- **Atomic element:** **A single hero image, a tiny block of caption text in a precise corner, nothing else above the fold.** No nav clutter, no scroll prompt, no badges. Caption acts like the title card of an art book.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Try a hero where the only text is a tight paragraph in the *bottom-right* corner. SMB owners read it as "this firm doesn't beg for attention."

### 2.4 Kengo Kuma & Associates — Project gallery
- **Source:** Kengo Kuma studio site / Siiimple feature
- **URL:** https://siiimple.com/kengo-kuma/
- **Atomic element:** **Large-format project photography on a generous, restrained grid** — usually 2–3 columns, each cell breathing, navigation labels as small text rather than buttons.
- **Why for OOPUO:** The case-studies index page could mirror this exactly: photo-led, no card chrome, no CTAs on tiles — the whole grid is the navigation.

### 2.5 Panasonic — Japanese craftsmanship as visual language
- **Source:** Panasonic global design page ("Japan Quality")
- **URL:** https://www.panasonic.com/global/consumer/jq.html
- **Atomic element:** **Material close-ups (a knob, a stitch, a hinge) used as section headers** instead of icons. A photo of *one detail* signals an entire value proposition.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Replace abstract icons with macro photography of the consultancy's actual artifacts — a contract page corner, a Linear board, a Notion doc spine.

## 3. Operations / Dashboard Aesthetics on Marketing Sites

We're stealing the marketing-page feel of these companies, not their products.

### 3.1 Linear.app — Monochrome with single-pixel hairlines
- **Source:** Linear brand guidelines
- **URL:** https://linear.app/brand · https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design/
- **Atomic element:** **Monochrome black/white palette** (the 2025 redesign moved further this direction) with section dividers as single-pixel hairlines, never thick rules. Inter font, ultra-tight numeric tracking.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Your nav and section breaks should be hairlines (0.5–1px) at low opacity, never bold rules — keeps the site feeling like a product spec.

### 3.2 Linear.app — Type scale with a "Meta" tier at 13px
- **Source:** Linear typography system documented at fontofweb.com
- **URL:** https://fontofweb.com/tokens/linear.app
- **Atomic element:** A **distinct "Meta" type tier at 13px** used for chrome, status text, and labels — sits clearly below body (16px) so the eye reads them as system text, not content.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Reserve a 12–13px JetBrains Mono "meta" tier for things like phase numbers, timestamp labels, and tile metadata. Don't promote it; it earns its quietness.

### 3.3 Vercel — The blueprint grid
- **Source:** Rauno Freiberg craft post · Geist grid component
- **URL:** https://rauno.me/craft/vercel · https://vercel.com/geist/grid
- **Atomic element:** A **subtle 1px grid at 10–20% opacity** stretched across hero, pricing, and feature sections — implemented by bordering every grid child on two adjacent sides (right + bottom). "Almost subliminal — you feel it before you see it."
- **Why for OOPUO:** The literal frame on every page reads as "infrastructure for thought" — exactly the mental model an SMB owner needs for an AI-systems consultancy.

### 3.4 Stripe — Connect cube header
- **Source:** Stripe engineering blog
- **URL:** https://stripe.com/blog/connect-front-end-experience
- **Atomic element:** **A small set of 3D cubes that rotate at random speeds**, sharing a single light source so faces illuminate consistently. The cubes are a *metaphor* for composition — building blocks of the product.
- **Why for OOPUO:** A small "module" object floating in the hero (cube, panel, card stack) rotating slowly under one consistent light source can stand in for "the systems we'll build for you." Not literal product chrome.

### 3.5 Stripe — Animated mesh gradient via minigl
- **Source:** Kevin Hufnagl breakdown · Stripe minigl
- **URL:** https://kevinhufnagl.com/how-to-stripe-website-gradient-effect/
- **Atomic element:** A **WebGL mesh gradient rendered through a ~10kb shader** that blends multiple color points across a canvas — feels almost three-dimensional, GPU-cheap. Not a video, not a static gradient.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Reserved for *one* moment (e.g., the "operations room coming online" transition) so the warm highlight has somewhere to bloom.

### 3.6 Datadog — Single pane of glass framing
- **Source:** Datadog product pages
- **URL:** https://www.datadoghq.com/product/platform/dashboards/ · https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/datadog-dashboards/
- **Atomic element:** A **responsive grid of small widget tiles inside one frame** — the marketing screenshot is always the *whole pane*, never one zoomed-in widget. Cohesion sells more than detail.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Show "the operations room" as one unified pane: even if the tiles are static, the *frame* is what convinces. Avoid hero shots of individual feature cards.

### 3.7 Grafana — "Telemetry waste" as headline
- **Source:** Grafana homepage
- **URL:** https://grafana.com/
- **Atomic element:** A headline that names a **specific quantified pain** ("Half of telemetry spend is wasted") immediately under a quiet hero — not a benefit, not a tagline, a *number*.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Replace abstract "AI for SMBs" hero copy with one quantified observation about the SMB's actual daily friction.

## 4. Spatial / Room-Based Navigation Marketing Sites

References for moving through a *place* instead of scrolling a page.

### 4.1 Apple — AirPods Pro scroll narrative
- **Source:** Apple product pages, documented in Awwwards & CSS-Tricks tutorial
- **URL:** https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/product-scroll-triggered-animation-apple-airpods-pro · https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/
- **Atomic element:** **A scroll-bound canvas that swaps frames against a pre-rendered image sequence**, hardware-accelerated via requestAnimationFrame. Scroll position = scrubbing a video, not parallax.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Use this for the "operations room booting up" moment only — a 60-frame sequence the user scrubs through to reveal panels lighting up. Once. Not on every section.

### 4.2 Bruno Simon — 3D drivable portfolio
- **Source:** bruno-simon.com (Awwwards Site of the Month)
- **URL:** https://bruno-simon.com/ · https://medium.com/@bruno_simon/bruno-simon-portfolio-case-study-960402cc259b
- **Atomic element:** **No interface chrome at all** — projects are physical objects you bump into. Three.js + Cannon.js physics. The choice to remove HUD entirely is the design.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Don't copy the 3D — copy the *removal*. One section of the site (e.g., the "approach" page) could have zero visible nav, just objects in space that respond to the cursor.

### 4.3 Igloo Inc — 3D scroll experience
- **Source:** Abeto / Awwwards Site of the Year 2024
- **URL:** https://www.awwwards.com/sites/igloo-inc · https://www.igloo.inc/
- **Atomic element:** A **continuous 3D environment that the scrollbar moves the camera through** — the site is one space, sections are camera positions. Built on Three.js + GSAP + Svelte.
- **Why for OOPUO:** This is the literal "operations room" pattern. Each marketing section = a camera dolly to a different console in the same room.

### 4.4 Active Theory — Fiomet portal transitions
- **Source:** Active Theory case study
- **URL:** https://medium.com/active-theory/fiomet-case-study-9a0180268423
- **Atomic element:** **Portal-like transitions between sections** — the next screen is "inside" a shape on the current screen, and clicking *enters* it. Implies depth without 3D engines.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Inter-page transitions where the destination page first appears as a small frame *inside* the current one, then expands. Sells the "rooms" metaphor cheaply.

### 4.5 Apple Vision Pro — Spatial browsing
- **Source:** visionOS HIG · Apple newsroom
- **URL:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-visionos · https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/06/introducing-apple-vision-pro/
- **Atomic element:** **Spatial scenes appear inline with text and come alive as you scroll**, with distractions hidden. The page becomes a stage where reading reveals scenes.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Long-form blog or case-study pages can hide chrome on entry and reveal small "dioramas" inline as the reader passes them. Subtle, not gimmicky.

## 5. Ambient Background Visual Systems

The persistent atmosphere that sells "this place is operational."

### 5.1 Stripe — minigl mesh gradient (atmosphere variant)
- **Source:** Kevin Hufnagl breakdown
- **URL:** https://kevinhufnagl.com/how-to-stripe-website-gradient-effect/
- **Atomic element:** A **slow-drifting WebGL gradient at low saturation** as ambient backdrop — the field is never static, but the motion is below conscious detection (single cycle ~60s+).
- **Why for OOPUO:** A barely-moving warm gradient behind the dark sections. Honors prefers-reduced-motion by going static, not by disappearing.

### 5.2 Vercel — Geist grid as persistent texture
- **Source:** Vercel Geist / Rauno's craft post
- **URL:** https://vercel.com/geist/grid
- **Atomic element:** **Grid lines at <20% opacity** carried across the entire site, not just hero sections. Implementation: borders on grid children, not a background image.
- **Why for OOPUO:** This is your light-mode primary atmosphere. Carries through every page so the site has one *substrate*.

### 5.3 Linear — Subtle dot/noise texture in dark sections
- **Source:** Linear marketing pages, documented in design analyses
- **URL:** https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design/
- **Atomic element:** **A faint noise/dot field on dark backgrounds** that breaks up flat black and prevents banding on gradients. Functional grain.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Apply 1–3% noise PNG over `bg-dark` sections. Cheap, GPU-free, instantly elevates the dark surfaces from "default black" to "considered surface."

### 5.4 NASA Apollo Mission Control — Big Boards
- **Source:** Apollo11Space deep-dive · Hackaday article
- **URL:** https://apollo11space.com/how-mission-controls-big-displays-worked-a-look-at-nasas-big-boards/ · https://hackaday.com/2020/10/29/a-look-behind-the-big-boards-at-mission-control-in-the-golden-age-of-nasa/
- **Atomic element:** **A wall divided into discrete sections, each owning one job** (trajectory, vitals, comms) with its own small heading. The whole wall is legible in one glance because the divisions are clean.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Footer or "system status" component as a wall of small labeled tiles — not a CTA garden, an operations board. Each tile has one label and one number.

### 5.5 Severance (Apple TV+) — Lumon Terminal aesthetic
- **Source:** Atomic Keyboard / Designboom on the "Lumon Terminal Pro" reveal
- **URL:** https://www.designboom.com/technology/lumon-terminal-pro-computer-tv-series-severance-apple-website-03-27-2025/ · https://trickle.so/blog/how-severance-uses-the-60-30-10-rule-to-inspire-ui-design
- **Atomic element:** **Monochromatic teal-on-darker-teal interface with a single accent color used at exactly the 10% spot** in the 60/30/10 rule. Pixel font reserved for terminal moments only.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Validates the "warm highlight max once per page" rule. The 60/30/10 split: 60% light surface, 30% dark accent, 10% teal — and the warm orange is *outside* the rule, used only at the final CTA.

### 5.6 Westworld — Delos tri-fold tablet UI
- **Source:** Chris Kieffer (Westworld FUI designer)
- **URL:** https://www.chriskieffer.com/posts/2016/12/16/westworld-fui · https://vanschneider.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-the-westworld-ui/
- **Atomic element:** **Sparse line-art panels with type that respects huge gutters** — hierarchy by spacing, not by weight. The interfaces look "real" because they don't try to look futuristic.
- **Why for OOPUO:** Whenever you're tempted to add another rule, badge, or icon — instead add gutter. Westworld's UI is a masterclass in restraint inside a sci-fi premise.

## 6. The "Evolving Environment" Pattern

Sites where the world *changes state* as the visitor moves through it. Three verified examples and how they evolve.

### 6.1 Apple — AirPods Pro page (frame-by-frame scrub)
- **URL:** https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/product-scroll-triggered-animation-apple-airpods-pro
- **How it evolves:** The hero **scrubs through a pre-rendered image sequence** as the user scrolls. The product rotates, opens, separates — environment evolves through *object state*, not background change. Implementation: HTML canvas + requestAnimationFrame, hardware-accelerated.
- **Borrow:** Reserve one section where scroll = scrubbing a sequence (e.g., the operations room "waking up" — dark panels light up tile by tile).

### 6.2 Igloo Inc — Camera dolly through one space
- **URL:** https://www.awwwards.com/sites/igloo-inc
- **How it evolves:** Built as a **single 3D environment**; scroll position drives a camera dolly path. Sections aren't pages — they're framings of the same space. Lighting and density shift as the camera moves through depth.
- **Borrow:** Even without 3D, treat scroll position as "where in the room you are" — backgrounds get warmer/cooler, density of background elements changes, but the surface stays continuous.

### 6.3 Stripe — Connect landing (light-source consistency)
- **URL:** https://stripe.com/blog/connect-front-end-experience
- **How it evolves:** All foreground objects (cubes, cards, illustrations) **share one global light source**. As you scroll past sections, the same light continues to define which faces are lit — gives the page a sense of being one *physical space* across the scroll.
- **Borrow:** Pick a light direction (e.g., "sun from upper-left at golden-hour angle") and apply it consistently to every illustration, shadow, and card across the entire site. No per-section relighting.

## TOP 10 ATOMIC ELEMENTS TO STEAL

Synthesized across all categories — these are the highest-leverage borrows for an SMB-targeting AI consultancy that needs to feel like an operations room without scaring off non-technical buyers.

1. **The persistent blueprint grid** (Vercel / Rauno Freiberg) — 1px lines at 10–15% opacity carried across every page. Implementation: borders on right + bottom of every grid child. This is the single biggest move toward "operations room" credibility.

2. **The single radial map as primary nav** (Destiny 2 Director) — replace the dropdown nav with one constellation view of the consultancy's services / case studies / approach. One screen, no IA tree.

3. **Center-empty hero composition** (Armored Core 6 garage) — vitals on the edges, hero subject in a deliberately empty middle. Looks serious without busy.

4. **60% emptiness rule on every tile** (MUJI / Kenya Hara's "ma") — image or content occupies less than half of any container. The whitespace is the design.

5. **Wide tracking on mono labels** (MUJI typography + Linear) — JetBrains Mono section headers at ~14px, ~0.12em letter-spacing. Reads "considered," not "developer-tools."

6. **Monochromatic line-art operations map** (Death Stranding cuff link) — a single-color contour map of the client's business state. Color of the *whole field* indicates state, not badges.

7. **Slow-drifting WebGL gradient on dark sections** (Stripe minigl) — single shader, ~10kb, motion below conscious detection. Static under prefers-reduced-motion.

8. **Globally consistent light source** (Stripe Connect cubes) — one light direction across every illustration, card shadow, and 3D element on the entire site. Sells "one physical space."

9. **Frame-scrubbed scroll moment, used exactly once** (Apple AirPods Pro) — reserve one canvas-driven scroll sequence for the "operations room coming online" reveal. Never on a second section.

10. **A "Meta" type tier at 13px** (Linear) — dedicated quiet tier for timestamps, phase numbers, system labels. Doesn't compete with body; earns its quietness by living below 16px and never being promoted.

## Anti-Patterns (do NOT borrow)

To keep the SMB owner trusting us:

- **Cyberpunk 2077 corner brackets / scanlines** — reads as cosplay
- **Bruno Simon literal 3D physics** — too playful for B2B
- **Severance pixel fonts** — borrow the palette discipline, not the typeface
- **Westworld tri-fold panels** — borrow the gutters, not the holographic chrome
- **Heavy parallax** — every example we found warns about overuse; use only the single scrub moment
